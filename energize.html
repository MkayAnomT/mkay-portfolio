01 · Overview

Fitness, organised by goal.

The original Energize site and its source were gone, so the product was rebuilt from nothing: information architecture first, then a design system, then a working React front end. The brief was clear about what to avoid: no generic gym template, no neon on black, no wall of identical cards.

The answer was an athletics track. Eight goals become eight lanes, numbered with stencil figures and staggered like a 400m start. The same lanes run through the whole product: the home page, the workout filters, onboarding and the footer. The final system runs on deep charcoal and graphite, with electric lime used only where it matters: actions, active lanes and progress.

02 · The problem

Libraries overwhelm. Goals focus.

Most fitness products open on a catalogue and leave people to work out where to begin. Energize answers two questions immediately: what is this, and what can I do here? Every next step is then one clear action, with no guessing about what happens after the tap.

Pick your lane

The hero is the product. Eight goals sit as staggered track lanes. Hovering one lights it, sends a runner mark to the finish and swaps the photo, and tapping it opens matching workouts.

Cards that help you choose

Every workout card shows time, level and kit before anything else. On mobile the rail scrolls sideways with snap points, and one tap saves a workout for later.

Start, with no surprises

The detail page shows the full exercise list and how the time splits across the session. Under the Start button, one line explains exactly what happens after you tap it.

A guided player

One exercise at a time, running on one clock. Timed moves count down by themselves and rep moves wait for Done. The next step is always previewed, and Space, ← and → work from the keyboard.

03 · Product screens

From discovery to finish line.

Discovery, programs, onboarding, progress and reading share one vocabulary of lanes, distance markers and finish lines. A finished session gets a check and a strike-through; the next session gets a flag.

04 · Mobile

Its own layout, not a shrunk desktop.

The menu is a full-height drawer of lanes with the main actions within thumb reach. Detail pages keep Start pinned to the bottom of the screen, and filters become swipeable chip rows. Every control is at least 44px, and the layout was tested from 320px to 1920px.

05 · Design process

Plan first, then pixels.

01

Structure

Information architecture, navigation, homepage hierarchy and the full flow: discover → detail → start → finish → log.

02

System

Tokens for colour, type, spacing, radius and motion. A condensed athletic display face, stencil numerals, square geometry and depth from overlap rather than shadow.

03

Build

Reusable React components (Navbar, Button, WorkoutCard, ProgramCard, ArticleCard, Testimonial, SectionHeader, Footer), with progress saved locally.

04

QA

Nine breakpoints, keyboard and focus states, reduced motion, empty states, a broken-link scan of 61 routes, and zero console errors.

06 · Key decisions

One metaphor, held all the way.

The track isn't decoration. It sets how people choose, how progress is shown and how each session ends.

01
Goals are the top-level vocabulary.

The eight lanes drive the hero, the workout filters, onboarding and the recommendation logic, so users learn one model once.

02
Every card answers time, level and kit.

Those are the three facts people use to decide, so they come first. Copy and imagery support them, never replace them.

03
Every CTA says what happens next.

“Start workout” opens a guided timer, and the page says so. Program pages always surface the next unfinished session.

04
Progress as marks, not points.

Finished sessions fill distance ticks and get struck through. Missing a day resets nothing: no guilt mechanics.

05
Honest content.

Member stories are labelled as samples, and every stat on the site is computed from the real product data. No invented user counts.
